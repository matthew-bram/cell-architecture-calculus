## Key Question 0 (the objective): Which NFR classes are properties of one trace, which are properties of sets of traces, and which logic fits each

### Takeaway
Timing deadlines, ordering, counts, "never after" and per-run response-time bounds are trace properties (safety or co-safety) and fit LTLf/MTL/MFOTL over a finite timestamped run; liveness and fairness are properties of infinite behaviours and can only be *inconclusively* judged on a finite run; confidentiality (noninterference, observational determinism), cross-run SLAs (mean response time over all executions), statistical/algorithmic fairness and robust cleanness are hyperproperties that no single-run Boolean predicate can express.

### Cited Findings
- Clarkson and Schneider define trace properties as sets of traces and hyperproperties as sets of sets of traces; "Important security policies cannot be expressed as properties of individual executions" and a "bound on mean response time over all executions is an availability policy that cannot" be a trace property **[source]** — [Clarkson & Schneider, Hyperproperties, JCS 2010 (Cornell PDF)](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/fbs/publications/Hyperproperties.pdf)
- SLAs: the paper lists "mean response time", "time service factor, the percentage of requests that are serviced within a specified time" and "percentage uptime", and states: "These statistics can be used to define policies with respect to individual executions of a system or across all executions of a system. In the former case, the SLA would be a trace property … 'The mean response time over all executions is less than 1 second'. This latter SLA is not a trace property, but it is a hyperproperty: RT ≜ {T ∈ Prop | mean(⋃_{t∈T} respTimes(t)) ≤ 1}" **[source]** — [same](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/fbs/publications/Hyperproperties.pdf)
- "Mean response time RT (2.7) is not liveness but it is hyperliveness … Note that if this policy were approximated by limiting the maximum response time in each execution, then the resulting hyperproperty would be a lifted safety property." **[source]** — [same](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/fbs/publications/Hyperproperties.pdf)
- k-safety: "A hyperproperty S is a k-safety hyperproperty (is k-safety) iff ∀T ∈ Prop: T ∉ S ⇒ (∃M ∈ Obs: M ≤ T ∧ |M| ≤ k ∧ (∀T′ ∈ Prop: M ≤ T′ ⇒ T′ ∉ S))"; "The 1-safety hyperproperties are the lifted safety properties"; observational determinism "is a 2-safety hyperproperty"; a secret-sharing policy over k shares is k-safety **[source]** — [same](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/fbs/publications/Hyperproperties.pdf)
- Lamport: a property P is a safety property iff "b doesn't satisfy P iff there is a prefix of b that doesn't satisfy P"; "<>(x = 3) is a liveness property; any finite sequence of states can be extended to a behavior satisfying it"; "Any property can be written as the conjunction of a safety property and a liveness property" (Alpern–Schneider) **[source]** — [Lamport, Safety, Liveness, and Fairness (2019)](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/tla/safety-liveness.pdf)
- Lamport: "If <<A>>_v is not equivalent to FALSE, then WF_v(A) and SF_v(A) are liveness properties", i.e. fairness is a liveness property, decided only on infinite behaviours **[source]** — [same](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/tla/safety-liveness.pdf)
- Bauer, Leucker, Schallhart: on a finite prefix the LTL3 verdict is "? otherwise" when "the so far observed prefix u itself is insufficient to determine how ϕ evaluates in any possible future continuation" **[source]** — [Bauer, Leucker, Schallhart, RV for LTL and TLTL, TOSEM 2011 (PDF)](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~bbonakda/teaching/CS745/papers/RV.pdf)
- Agrawal and Bonakdarpour, citing Pnueli–Zaks: "LTL formula GFp is not monitorable, since there is no way to tell at run time, whether or not in the future, p will be visited infinitely often. On the contrary, formulas in LTL_S (e.g., Gp) and in LTL_C (e.g., Fp) are monitorable." **[source]** — [Agrawal & Bonakdarpour, RV of k-Safety Hyperproperties in HyperLTL, CSF 2016 (PDF)](https://www.cse.msu.edu/tart/sites/default/files/publications/files/2023-10/csf16.pdf)
- Dwyer's catalogue: Response globally is `[](P -> <>S)`; Absence globally `[](!P)`; Bounded Existence "at most 2 occurrences" globally is `(!P W (P W (!P W (P W []!P))))` **[source]** — [Dwyer et al., Property Specification Patterns, LTL mappings](https://doi.org/10.1145/302405.302672)
- Autili, Grunske, Lumpe, Pelliccione, Tang (TSE 41(7), 2015) align "qualitative, real-time and probabilistic patterns" with "specification support through a structured English grammar"; the catalogue's real-time patterns include Minimum Duration and Maximum Duration **[summary]** — [ps-patterns.wikidot.com](http://ps-patterns.wikidot.com/); the full real-time set (bounded response, bounded recurrence, bounded invariance, min/max duration) **[memory]**
- Cirstea, Kuppe, Loillier, Merz: "Since in this work we only analyze finite traces of programs, we ignore liveness properties and are interested in finite behaviors." **[source]** — [arXiv 2404.16075](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16075)
- Robust cleanness (software doping) is a hyperproperty "needs both universal and existential quantifications of system trajectories" and is characterised in HyperSTL **[summary]** — [Biewer et al., Conformance Relations and Hyperproperties for Doping Detection, LMCS 2022 (arXiv 2012.03910)](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.03910)
- Henzinger et al. monitor algorithmic fairness (demographic parity, equal opportunity) as quantitative estimates over a long sequence of events from an unknown Markov-chain system **[summary]** — [Monitoring Algorithmic Fairness, CAV 2023 (arXiv 2305.15979)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15979)

### Inferences
- Classification table (synthesised from the above):

| NFR class | Trace property or hyperproperty | Safety / liveness | Fitting logic | On one recorded run |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Timing deadline ("B within d of A") | trace | bounded response: co-safety once A seen, safety overall (violated when the deadline passes) | MTL / MLTL `G(A → F_[0,d] B)`; MFOTL with data; STL robustness gives slack | decidable, two-valued once the run extends past the deadline; LTLf weak/strong next at the end |
| Ordering ("B never before A", "A precedes B") | trace | safety | LTL(f) precedence/response patterns (Dwyer) | decidable |
| Count ("at most n of X per run") | trace | safety | LTL bounded existence; MFOTL `CNT` aggregation | decidable |
| Count per window ("at most n per hour") | trace | safety | MFOTL with aggregation over `ONCE[0,3600]`; RTLola sliding window `count`; MLTL cannot count natively | decidable |
| "Never after" | trace | safety | LTL `G(fence → G ¬later)` | decidable |
| Liveness ("eventually responds", no bound) | trace | liveness | LTL `G(A → F B)` | inconclusive on a finite prefix (LTL3 `?`); only refutable with a bound |
| Fairness (WF/SF, `GF`) | trace | liveness | TLA+ WF/SF, LTL `GF` | non-monitorable (Pnueli–Zaks) |
| Mean response time across executions, p99 latency over all runs | hyperproperty | hyperliveness | Clarkson–Schneider set-level definition; no HyperLTL formula with a finite alternation (needs aggregation over sets) | needs many runs; per-run bound is a lifted safety property |
| Confidentiality (noninterference, observational determinism) | hyperproperty | 2-safety (OD), hyperliveness (GNI) | HyperLTL `∀π∀π′ …` | needs ≥2 runs; k-safety is monitorable over sets of finite traces |
| Statistical / algorithmic fairness | hyperproperty / quantitative over distributions | — | Henzinger et al.'s specification language over Markov chains | needs a long run or many runs; verdict is an estimate with confidence |
| Robustness (CPS, "how much") | trace, quantitative | — | STL robustness `ρ ∈ ℝ` | computable on one run |
| Robustness as insensitivity to small input changes (robust cleanness) | hyperproperty | — | HyperSTL | needs pairs of runs |

- The reader's three predicates are, respectively, instances of bounded response (`withinDuration`), bounded existence/counting (`atMostCount`) and the Dwyer "Absence after Q" pattern `[](Q -> [](!P))` (`neverAfter`), evaluated with LTLf's finite-trace semantics. All three are safety properties, hence correctly two-valued on a terminated run.

### Gaps
- I did not find a source that classifies "fairness" (in the scheduling sense) as k-safety; Lamport and Agrawal–Bonakdarpour both place `GF`-style fairness in the non-monitorable liveness class. The phrase "fairness as k-safety" in the brief most likely refers to *algorithmic* fairness compared across individuals/groups, which Henzinger et al. treat statistically rather than as a k-safety HyperLTL formula.

## Key Question 1: LTL, LTLf, MTL and STL — what each expresses, and STL's robustness as "how much"

### Takeaway
LTL speaks about infinite discrete-time traces; LTLf re-interprets it over finite nonempty traces by splitting Next into strong and weak forms; MTL adds interval bounds on temporal operators over timestamped words (decidable but non-primitive-recursive over finite words, MITL restricts to nonsingular intervals for EXPSPACE); STL is MITL over real-valued signals, and its robustness semantics (Fainekos–Pappas in space, Donzé–Maler in time) replaces the Boolean verdict with a real number whose sign agrees with satisfaction and whose magnitude is the margin.

### Cited Findings
- De Giacomo and Vardi, "Linear Temporal Logic and Linear Dynamic Logic on Finite Traces", IJCAI 2013, pp. 854–860: LTLf is "less expressive than might initially appear" and the paper proposes LDLf with PDL syntax over finite traces **[summary]** — [dblp](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/conf/ijcai/GiacomoV13.html); [Rice repository](https://repository.rice.edu/items/08ed8c8b-2c03-4e0e-9bea-f06fc9ca5dd3)
- LTLf's end-of-trace treatment, as described in the LeanLTL paper: "The two resulting operators are strong next, with Xs p (○p in [6]) evaluating to false at the end of a finite trace, and weak next, with Xw p (originally ∼○p) evaluating to true at the end of a finite trace; it is equivalent to (Xs p) ∨ ¬Xs ⊤." and "we require traces to be nonempty, as in LTLf" **[source]** — [Vin et al., LeanLTL, ITP 2025 (arXiv 2507.01780)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01780)
- MTL was introduced by Koymans (1990) **[summary/memory]**; MITL (Alur, Feder, Henzinger) restricts temporal operators to "nonsingular intervals" and gives an EXPSPACE decision procedure via timed automata **[summary]** — [Ouaknine & Worrell, On the decidability of MTL, LICS 2005](http://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/james.worrell/lics05.pdf)
- "Over finite timed words both model checking and satisfiability for MTL are decidable but not primitive recursive" **[summary]** — [Ouaknine & Worrell, survey 2008](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/james.worrell/mtlsurvey08.pdf)
- Donzé and Maler (FORMATS 2010): "We present several variants of robustness measures that indicate how far a given trajectory stands, in space and time, from satisfying or violating a property"; they cite Fainekos and Pappas for the "robustness degree" (space) and add "an alternative quantitative semantics for MITL/STL which focuses on the robustness of satisfaction with respect to time", then "a generalized robustness measure which captures both space and time, from which both the space robustness of [11] and our time robustness are obtained as special cases" **[source]** — [Donzé & Maler PDF](http://www-verimag.imag.fr/~maler/Papers/sensiform.pdf); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-15297-9_9)
- Donzé and Maler note that timed behaviours "can be viewed as either Boolean signals … or as timed words consisting of instantaneous events, taken from some alphabet, separated by real time durations" and that MTL/MITL "can express requirements concerning the time elapsing between events" **[source]** — [same PDF](http://www-verimag.imag.fr/~maler/Papers/sensiform.pdf)
- Fainekos and Pappas, TCS 410(42) 2009: "robust interpretation of Metric Temporal Logic (MTL) formulas over signals that take values in metric spaces", computing a robustness estimate for finite timed state sequences and relating discrete-time to continuous-time estimates **[summary]** — [ASU record](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/robustness-of-temporal-logic-specifications-for-continuous-time-s/)
- Deshmukh et al., "Robust online monitoring of signal temporal logic", FMSD 2017, compute robustness intervals online **[summary]** — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10703-017-0286-7)
- STL robustness recursion (standard form): ρ(f(x) ≥ 0, w, t) = f(w(t)); ρ(¬φ) = −ρ(φ); ρ(φ∧ψ) = min; ρ(φ∨ψ) = max; ρ(F_I φ, w, t) = sup_{t′∈t+I} ρ(φ, w, t′); ρ(G_I φ) = inf; U_I by sup of min; soundness: ρ > 0 ⇒ satisfaction, ρ < 0 ⇒ violation **[memory]**
- Bauer, Leucker, Schallhart define TLTL (Raskin's event-clock timed LTL) with "event recording and event predicting clocks" and give it a three-valued semantics TLTL3 on finite timed words **[source]** — [TOSEM PDF](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~bbonakda/teaching/CS745/papers/RV.pdf)
- MLTL (R2U2): "a variant of LTL with closed integer-bounded intervals on the temporal operators", chosen because it "excels at capturing requirements conceptualized as timelines" **[source]** — [Johannsen et al., R2U2 v3.0, CAV 2023 (PDF)](https://research.temporallogic.org/papers/JJKRZ23.pdf)

### Inferences
- For the reader's model (events with `issuedAt`/`recordedAt` in integer milliseconds), the natural logic is pointwise MTL/MFOTL over a finite timed word, with LTLf end-of-trace semantics: `withinDuration` is `G(root → F_[0,limit] target)` read with `recordedAt` as the timestamp of `target` and `issuedAt` as the timestamp of `root`.
- STL robustness transfers directly to timing NFRs even though the data are discrete: for `G(latency ≤ 200)` over a run, ρ = 200 − max latency is the slack in milliseconds; for `F_[0,d] target`, ρ_time = d − (recordedAt − issuedAt) is Donzé–Maler's time robustness. With integer milliseconds this needs only `Int` min/max, not reals.
- The LTLf strong/weak-next distinction is what the reader's `neverAfter` already handles implicitly (`find?` returning `none` makes the property vacuously true, which is the weak reading).

## Key Question 2: Runtime verification — monitors from formulas, three/four-valued verdicts, tools, and counts/aggregations over windows

### Takeaway
RV synthesises a monitor (automaton or stream program) from a temporal formula and runs it over a finite prefix, giving ⊤/⊥ only when every continuation agrees and `?` otherwise (LTL3; RV-LTL refines `?` into "presumably true/false"); counting and windowed aggregation ("at most n per hour") are handled natively by MonPoly/VeriMon (MFOTL with SQL-like aggregations) and by stream languages (RTLola sliding windows, Copilot), while MLTL-based R2U2 handles bounded intervals and only limited set aggregation.

### Cited Findings
- Leucker and Schallhart, "A brief account of runtime verification", J. Logic and Algebraic Programming 78(5), 2009 **[summary]** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1567832608000775). (Note: Havelund is not an author of this paper; the brief's attribution is off. Havelund's relevant work is DejaVu / PyDejaVu on first-order past-time temporal logic monitoring **[summary]** — [Havelund et al. 2025 PDF](https://www.havelund.com/Publications/cb-pydejavu-2025.pdf).)
- LTL3 semantics (Bauer, Leucker, Schallhart, TOSEM 2011, Def. 2.4): "[u ⊨ ϕ] = ⊤ if ∀σ ∈ Σ^ω: uσ ⊨ ϕ; ⊥ if ∀σ ∈ Σ^ω: uσ ⊭ ϕ; ? otherwise"; monitor construction goes "Formula → NBA → Emptiness per state → NFA → DFA → FSM" (Fig. 1); "the set of monitorable properties does not only encompass the safety and co-safety" properties; the paper evaluates "The 54 monitorable LTL specification patterns" of Dwyer's catalogue **[source]** — [TOSEM PDF](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~bbonakda/teaching/CS745/papers/RV.pdf)
- RV-LTL (Bauer, Leucker, Schallhart, RV 2007): four truth values "true, false, possibly true, possibly false", a Moore-machine monitor **[summary]** — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-77395-5_11)
- Bartocci, Falcone, Francalanza, Reger, "Introduction to Runtime Verification", in Lectures on RV, LNCS 10457, pp. 1–33, 2018: "primer … overview of the main specification languages used for RV … the monitorability problem" **[summary]** — [TU Wien record](https://repositum.tuwien.at/handle/20.500.12708/29839)
- MonPoly: "policy specification language based on metric first-order temporal logic (MFOTL)"; "its policy language includes operators for aggregating data values"; Basin et al. "extended MFOTL with SQL-like aggregation operators … partitions the satisfying assignments of its subformula into groups, and then computes a summary value, such as count, sum, or average, for each group" **[summary]** — [The MonPoly Monitoring Tool (EasyChair)](https://easychair.org/publications/paper/62MC/download); [JACM 62(2) 2015](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2699444)

- VeriMon (AFP `MFOTL_Monitor`, Schneider & Traytel, 2019): "decides whether the policy is satisfied at every point in the stream" of timestamped events, "a simplified variant of the MonPoly tool's algorithm", and "this case study revealed several errors in the optimized but unverified tools"; related entries `MFODL_Monitor_Optimized` ("for metric first-order dynamic logic with aggregations") and `MFOTL_Checker` **[source]** — [AFP entry](https://www.isa-afp.org/entries/MFOTL_Monitor.html)
- RTLola: "sliding windows over real-time intervals with aggregation functions"; example `gps.aggregate(over:2s, using:count)`; aggregation functions "exists, avg, sum, count, min or max" **[summary]** — [Faymonville et al., Real-time Stream-based Monitoring (arXiv 1711.03829)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.03829)
- R2U2: "given a runtime requirement ϕ and an input computation π of sensor and software values at each timestamp i, R2U2 returns the verdict (true or false) for all i as to whether π,i ⊨ ϕ"; "It also runs offline, over simulated data streams or recorded data logs"; Assume-Guarantee Contracts give "three-valued verdicts"; C2PO supports "set aggregation operators" and future work names "foratmostn" **[source]** — [R2U2 v3.0 PDF](https://research.temporallogic.org/papers/JJKRZ23.pdf); flight-certified status (Copilot, LOLA, R2U2) **[summary]** — [R2U2 docs](https://r2u2.github.io/r2u2/)
- Copilot: "supports temporal logic (LTL, PTLTL and MTL), clocks and voting algorithms"; generates C99 whose "output is constant in memory and time"; used at "NASA Langley Research Center for monitoring test flights of drones" **[summary]** — [copilot-language.github.io](https://copilot-language.github.io/)
- Monitoring arithmetic temporal properties on finite traces (Felli, Montali, Winkler, AAAI 2023) extends LTLf monitoring with arithmetic constraints over data **[summary]** — [arXiv 2211.17166](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.17166)

### Inferences
- Checking a *recorded* run is offline monitoring: the same monitor consumes the log; for a terminated run with LTLf semantics the verdict is two-valued, and the `?` verdict only arises when the run is treated as a prefix of a longer behaviour. The reader's `RunPromise` is the LTLf/offline reading.
- For "at most n per hour" over the reader's `List Event`, the MFOTL pattern maps to a Lean predicate: for every event `e`, the count of target events `e'` with `e.recordedAt.ms − 3600000 ≤ e'.recordedAt.ms ≤ e.recordedAt.ms` is `≤ n` — a double `List.all`/`filter`, exactly VeriMon's "finite relations" approach specialised to a list.
- MLTL/R2U2 are the wrong fit for counts over data-carrying events; MFOTL (MonPoly/VeriMon) or stream languages with windows (RTLola/Copilot) are the fit.

## Key Question 3: Hyperproperties — which NFRs are hyperproperties, why a single-run predicate cannot express them, and how they are monitored

### Takeaway
Noninterference, observational determinism, cross-execution SLAs (mean response time), robust cleanness and distributional fairness are hyperproperties because their violation witness is a *set* of traces (a pair for 2-safety); HyperLTL adds trace quantifiers to LTL, and monitoring is possible for the alternation-free (∀*) fragment over growing sets of finite traces, with ∀∃ formulas being non-monitorable.

### Cited Findings
- Clarkson–Schneider: "GNI is not a trace property because the presence of any two traces t1 and t2 in a" system is what matters; "OD is not a trace property because whether some trace is" allowed depends on the other traces **[source]** — [Hyperproperties PDF](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/fbs/publications/Hyperproperties.pdf)
- "Observational determinism OD (2.6) is hypersafety. The bad thing is a pair of" traces; "Theorem 2 provides a verification technique for k-safety: reduce a k-safety hyperproperty K of S to a safety property K′ of S^k" (self-composition) **[source]** — [same](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/fbs/publications/Hyperproperties.pdf)
- Hyperliveness: "Hyperproperty L is a liveness hyperproperty (is hyperliveness) iff ∀T ∈ Obs: (∃T′ ∈ Prop: T ≤ T′ ∧ T′ ∈ L)"; "The only hyperproperty that is both hypersafety and hyperliveness is true" **[source]** — [same](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/fbs/publications/Hyperproperties.pdf)
- HyperLTL (Clarkson, Finkbeiner, Koleini, Micinski, Rabe, Sánchez, POST 2014): "Standard temporal logics can only refer to a single path at a time, and therefore cannot express many hyperproperties of interest, including noninterference"; "The model checking problem … is decidable. For alternation depth 1, the complexity is PSPACE in the length of the formula and NLOGSPACE in the size of the system, as for linear-time temporal logic." **[summary (Semantic Scholar abstract)]** — [Springer DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-54792-8_15](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-54792-8_15)
- Agrawal–Bonakdarpour (CSF 2016): three-valued semantics of a HyperLTL formula over a finite set M of finite traces: "⊤ if ∀T ∈ P(Σ^ω).(M ≤ T). Π ⊨_T ϕ; ⊥ if ∀T … Π ⊭_T ϕ; ? otherwise"; "HyperLTL_n formulas, where n ≥ 2, (e.g., ∀∃ψ) are not monitorable, as evaluating such formulas requires one to have all traces of the system"; the k-share secret-sharing formula "∀π1···∀πk.(G¬a1(π1,…,πk) ∨ … ∨ G¬ak(π1,…,πk))" "is monitorable"; "Theorem 2. Every k-safety hyperproperty and every co-k-safety hyperproperty that satisfies Theorem 1 is monitorable" **[source]** — [CSF 2016 PDF](https://www.cse.msu.edu/tart/sites/default/files/publications/files/2023-10/csf16.pdf)
- Finkbeiner, Hahn, Stenger, Tentrup, "Monitoring hyperproperties", FMSD 54(3) 2019: three input models — "the parallel model where a fixed number of system executions is processed in parallel; the unbounded sequential model, where system executions are processed sequentially one execution at a time; and the bounded sequential model" **[summary]** — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10703-019-00334-z)
- RVHyper: "rewriting-based technique for runtime verification of the full alternation-free fragment of HyperLTL" **[summary]** — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/RVHyper:-A-Runtime-Verification-Tool-for-Temporal-Finkbeiner-Hahn/c4fb43a4d3c65690947790acd0722417834225ec)
- Henzinger et al.: runtime monitors that "output quantitative estimates after each observation" for "demographic parity, equal opportunity, and social burden", assuming a Markov-chain system **[summary]** — [arXiv 2305.15979](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15979); extended to dynamic fairness and partial observation **[summary]** — [arXiv 2305.04699](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.04699)
- Robust cleanness: "for every pair of input prefixes on which no difference in the inputs exceeding a threshold has occurred so far, the corresponding sets of output prefixes are also conformant with respect to an output threshold"; characterised in HyperSTL **[summary]** — [arXiv 2012.03910](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.03910)

### Inferences
- A `RunPromise : Run → Bool` is closed under lifting to `List Run → Bool` by `all`, but that only yields lifted (1-safety) properties. A 2-safety property such as observational determinism needs a predicate of type `Run → Run → Bool` quantified over all pairs of runs that agree on low inputs; mean-response-time-over-all-runs needs `List Run → Bool` that is *not* an `all` of a per-run predicate. The reader's `kept : List Run → Nat × Nat` ("how many of the runs kept it, over how many were seen") is already a hyperproperty-shaped measure: a threshold on it ("≥ 99 % of runs within d") is Clarkson–Schneider's "time service factor" and is hyperliveness, not a lifted trace property.
- Monitoring a 2-safety property over recorded runs is feasible with Agrawal–Bonakdarpour's set semantics: check every pair in the recorded set; a violating pair is a permanent ⊥.

### Gaps
- No source found treating scheduling fairness as a k-safety hyperproperty (see Key Question 0).

## Key Question 4: TLA+ liveness and fairness, trace validation against TLA+ specs, and Quint

### Takeaway
TLA+ states liveness as fairness conjuncts WF_v(A)/SF_v(A) over infinite, stuttering-insensitive behaviours; Cirstea–Kuppe–Loillier–Merz validate *finite* recorded traces by turning the trace into a constraint on TLC's state exploration (a reachability/safety check that deliberately ignores liveness and does not check refinement), and Quint exposes runs as first-class test artefacts via the ITF format while supporting temporal properties only through TLC.

### Cited Findings
- Lamport: "An action formula A is a predicate on steps"; "the action <<A>>_v is defined to equal A /\ (v′ /= v)"; WF_v(A) holds iff "If b contains a suffix all of whose states satisfy ENABLED <<A>>_v, then b contains infinitely many <<A>>_v steps"; SF_v(A) iff "If b contains infinitely many states satisfying ENABLED <<A>>_v, then it contains infinitely many <<A>>_v steps"; "Any behavior satisfying SF_v(A) also satisfies WF_v(A)"; "Every property that can be written in TLA+ is stuttering insensitive" **[source]** — [Lamport, Safety, Liveness, and Fairness](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/tla/safety-liveness.pdf)
- Cirstea, Kuppe, Loillier, Merz, SEFM 2024: framework "consists of an API for instrumenting Java programs in order to record traces of executions, of a collection of TLA+ operators that are used for relating those traces to specifications, and of scripts for running the model checker"; the problem "is reduced to a constrained model checking problem, realized using the TLC model checker" **[summary]** — [arXiv 2404.16075](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16075)
- Trace lines are JSON such as `{"clock": 44, "rmState": [{"op": "Update", "path": ["rm-0"], "args": ["prepared"]}]}` with optional event name and arguments; the library "provides different types of clocks (in-memory, …)" and "currently supports scalar logical clocks but vector clocks can easily be added" **[source]** — [arXiv PDF v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.16075v2)
- The `TraceSpec` module: `IsEvent(e) == l ∈ 1..Len(Trace) ∧ ("event" ∈ DOMAIN Trace[l] ⇒ Trace[l].event = e) ∧ l′ = l + 1 ∧ UpdateVariables(Trace[l])`; `TraceAccepted == Len(Trace) = TLCGet("stats").diameter − 1`; "post-condition TraceAccepted ensures that at least one matching behavior was found" **[source]** — [Merz, TLA+ Community Meeting 2024 slides](https://conf.tlapl.us/2024-fm/slides-merz.pdf)
- "It would also be inappropriate to verify the liveness property ◇(l ≥ Len(Trace)), which would not hold for incomplete prefixes of the constrained state space"; "we do not check for refinement of the high-level specification by the trace … this condition would be too strong in the presence of non-deterministically chosen values"; a timeout "conceptually corresponds to a stuttering step of the specification" **[source]** — [arXiv PDF v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.16075v2)
- Case studies: two-phase commit (implementation counted duplicate "Prepared" messages twice → premature commit), EWD 998, a key-value store, Raft, and CCF, where trace validation "discovered serious violations of key safety properties" later confirmed by new tests **[summary (fetch of arXiv HTML)]** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.16075v2)
- Quint: "Temporal properties … can only be checked with quint verify --backend=tlc (Apalache has partial support, and the Quint simulator doesn't support them)"; Quint treats "a run as a first-class citizen, which is a concrete execution path (sequence of actions) that can serve as a test or test pattern"; "the ITF trace format" is consumed by "Quint Connect … to drive model-based tests against your implementation" **[summary]** — [quint.sh FAQ](https://quint.sh/faq)
- PGo "Automating Trace Validation" (Hackett, Beschastnikh, TLA+ Conf 2025) and OOPSLA 2025 "TraceLink" continue this line **[summary]** — [slides](https://conf.tlapl.us/2025-etaps/hackett.pdf); [TraceLink PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~bestchai/papers/oopsla25-trace-link.pdf)

### Inferences
- TLA+ trace validation is a *membership* check (is this finite trace a prefix of some behaviour of Spec?), which is a safety check; it is complementary to the reader's predicate approach, which checks a property of the trace rather than conformance to a model. Both are silent on liveness/fairness by construction.
- The reader's `issuedAt`/`recordedAt` pair plays the role of Cirstea et al.'s `clock`; their need to "merge traces of individual processes and sort them by timestamps" (Merz slides) is the same ordering assumption the reader's `neverAfter` makes by comparing `recordedAt.ms`.

### Gaps
- The Quint docs page on GitHub returned 503; details on fairness syntax in Quint (`weakFair`/`strongFair`) are from memory and not confirmed here.

## Key Question 5: Mechanization in Lean 4, Coq/Rocq and Isabelle, and what a metric property over a finite timestamped list takes in Lean 4 core

### Takeaway
Isabelle has the only verified *metric first-order* monitors (AFP `MFOTL_Monitor`, `MFODL_Monitor_Optimized` with aggregations and a sliding-window algorithm, `MFOTL_Checker`); Lean 4 has three LTL libraries — LeanLTL (finite and infinite traces, LTLf embedding, Mathlib-dependent), Lentil (TLA-style LTL proof mode, depends only on aesop) and LeanearTemporalLogic (LTL with finite traces, no metric operators) — none metric; Coq has coq-tla (TLA with WF/leads-to), the Coupet-Grimal coinductive CiC axiomatization, and LTL_Coq/coq-cds4ltl, also none metric. A pointwise-MTL/LTLf evaluator over `List Event` with `Nat` timestamps needs nothing beyond Lean core (`List`, `Nat`, `Int`, `decide`, `omega`), which the reader's `Promise.lean` already demonstrates.

### Cited Findings
- LeanLTL (Vin et al., ITP 2025, LIPIcs 37): `structure Trace (σ : Type*) where toFun? : ℕ → Option σ; length : ℕ∞; nempty : 0 < length; defined : ∀ i : ℕ, i < length ↔ (toFun? i).isSome` — "This models both finite and infinite traces"; `structure TraceSet (σ) where sat : Trace σ → Prop`; `structure TraceFun (σ α) where eval : Trace σ → Option α`; "Formalized proofs that LTL and LTLf can be embedded into LeanLTL"; "This type is equivalent to Set (Trace σ), using Set from Lean's Mathlib" **[source]** — [arXiv 2507.01780](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01780); [LIPIcs](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITP.2025.37)
- LeanLTL's `lakefile.toml` has `[[require]] name = "mathlib" scope = "leanprover-community"`, and its README lists `LeanLTL/Logics/LTL.lean` and `LTLf.lean` **[source]** — [GitHub UCSCFormalMethods/LeanLTL](https://github.com/UCSCFormalMethods/LeanLTL)
- Lentil (verse-lab): "formalizes the semantics of LTL, provides rules and a dedicated proof mode to conduct proofs in LTL as an object logic"; `lakefile.lean` requires only `aesop` (v4.34.0); definitions "initially ported from coq-tla"; proof mode "inspired by Iris Proof Mode"; Apache 2.0 **[source]** — [GitHub verse-lab/Lentil](https://github.com/verse-lab/Lentil)
- LeanearTemporalLogic (Pawagi): `InfiniteTrace` and `FiniteTrace` over sets of atomic propositions; operators next/until plus derived; duality, expansion and fixpoint lemmas; "finite traces are prefixes" results; no metric/bounded operators **[summary]** — [GitHub](https://github.com/mrigankpawagi/LeanearTemporalLogic)
- coq-tla (Chajed): "Embedding TLA in Coq" for "liveness reasoning", with WF and leads-to rules, following Lamport's TLA paper **[summary]** — [GitHub tchajed/coq-tla](https://github.com/tchajed/coq-tla)
- Coq axiomatization of LTL in CiC: "co-inductive representation of program executions, with temporal operators implemented as co-inductive (respectively inductive) types when they are greatest (respectively least) fixpoints" (Coupet-Grimal) **[summary]** — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220388389_An_Axiomatization_of_Linear_Temporal_Logic_in_the_Calculus_of_Inductive_Constructions); LTL_Coq **[summary]** — [GitHub](https://github.com/spidermoy/LTL_Coq); coq-cds4ltl (Rocq, 240+ theorems of Warford–Vega–Staley's calculational system) **[summary]** — [GitHub](https://github.com/jwiegley/coq-cds4ltl)
- Isabelle AFP `MFOTL_Monitor` (Schneider, Traytel, 4 July 2019; RV 2019 paper by Schneider, Basin, Krstić, Traytel): verified monitor that "decides whether the policy is satisfied at every point in the stream" **[source]** — [AFP](https://www.isa-afp.org/entries/MFOTL_Monitor.html)
- Basin, Dardinier, Heimes, Krstić, Raszyk, Schneider, Traytel, IJCAR 2020: "correctness proof in Isabelle/HOL of a monitor for metric first-order dynamic logic … supporting aggregations, regular expressions (the dynamic part), and optimizations including multi-way joins adopted from databases and a new sliding window algorithm" **[summary (Semantic Scholar abstract)]** — [Springer DOI 10.1007/978-3-030-51074-9_25](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-51074-9_25). (Correction to the brief: "A formally verified monitor for MFOTL" (RV 2019) is by Schneider, Basin, Krstić and Traytel; Dardinier is a co-author of the 2020 MFODL paper.)
- VeriMon+ "closes all expressiveness gaps between MonPoly and VeriMon", supporting "aggregation operators like sum and average, arbitrary negations of closed formulas, the unbounded Next operator, and constraints involving terms" **[summary]** — [VeriMon: A Formally Verified Monitoring Tool, ICTAC 2022](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/364097723_VeriMon_A_Formally_Verified_Monitoring_Tool)
- "Verified First-Order Monitoring with Recursive Rules" (TACAS 2022) extends the Isabelle line to Datalog-style rules **[summary]** — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-99527-0_13)
- The reader's `Promise.lean` already proves `withinDuration_mono` with `simp only [withinDuration, Bool.or_eq_true, Bool.not_eq_true', List.any_eq_true, Bool.and_eq_true, decide_eq_true_eq]` and `omega` and the package has no dependencies **[source: repository]**

### Inferences
- What it takes in Lean 4 core, no Mathlib (based on the libraries above and the reader's file):
  1. **Trace type**: `List Event` with `Event` carrying `recordedAt.ms : Nat` (already present). Nonemptiness (LTLf) is the `root` of the `Run`. A monotonicity hypothesis `Pairwise (fun a b => a.recordedAt.ms ≤ b.recordedAt.ms)` (core has `List.Pairwise`, `List.Sorted` is Mathlib) is needed for anything using `find?` as "first".
  2. **Formula type**: `inductive MTL (Event) | atom (Event → Bool) | neg | and | nextS | nextW | until (lo hi : Nat) | since (lo hi : Nat)`; this is Dwyer/Autili-complete for bounded patterns, and the pointwise-MTL interval `[lo,hi]` is read on `recordedAt.ms` differences.
  3. **Semantics**: `def sat : MTL → List Event → Nat → Prop` by structural recursion on the formula with positions as `Nat` indices (`List.get?`/`l[i]?`), bounded quantification `∃ j, i ≤ j ∧ j < l.length ∧ …`; core provides `Nat.lt_wfRel`, `List.length`, `List.drop`, `List.getElem?`.
  4. **Evaluator**: `def eval : MTL → List Event → Nat → Bool` mirroring `sat`, using `List.any`/`List.all` over `List.range` or over the suffix `l.drop i`; prove `eval_iff_sat` by induction on the formula with `List.any_eq_true`, `List.all_eq_true`, `decide_eq_true_eq` — the same lemma set as `withinDuration_mono`. Then `decide` or `native_decide` discharges closed instances (fixtures) and theorems such as `withinDuration_mono` generalise to "loosening any interval bound preserves satisfaction".
  5. **Aggregation**: `CNT` over a window is `((l.filter fun e' => inWindow e e' ∧ isTarget e').length ≤ n)`, VeriMon's finite-relation approach specialised to lists; `SUM`/`AVG` need `Nat`/`Int` only.
  6. **Robustness** (STL-style): `def rho : MTL → List Event → Nat → Int` with `min`/`max` on `Int` and the atom's margin (e.g. `limit − (recordedAt − issuedAt)`); soundness `rho > 0 → sat` is provable in core by the same induction. Real-valued signals are the only reason to reach for Mathlib's `ℝ`.
  7. **Three-valued prefix verdicts**: the LTL3 definition quantifies over infinite continuations and is not directly computable; for a recorded, terminated run the LTLf two-valued semantics is the right one, and a `Verdict := yes | no | unknown` is only needed if runs are treated as open prefixes (then `unknown` is returned for `F`/`U` whose window has not yet elapsed — computable because the window is bounded by `hi`).
- Neither LeanLTL nor Lentil needs to be imported for this: LeanLTL's `Trace` is `ℕ → Option σ` with an `ℕ∞` length and requires Mathlib; a `List` is the finite special case and the reader already works there. LeanLTL is the reference if infinite behaviours (liveness/fairness) ever need stating.
- No Mathlib or Lean core module formalising LTL was found; the three libraries are community projects (UCSC, NUS verse-lab, one individual).

### Gaps
- I did not find any Lean 4 library with metric/bounded temporal operators or with a verified monitor; nor any Coq library with MTL. The Isabelle VeriMon papers themselves could not be fetched (ETH/TUM links refused), so fragment details are from the AFP page and abstracts.
- Lentil's reference manual (operator list, finite-trace support) was not read; its README only says "LTL".

## Key Question 6: Given/When/Then examples versus temporal properties; scenario-based specification literature

### Takeaway
A Given/When/Then example is one finite trace (a witness), and the corresponding requirement is its universal closure over all runs; Live Sequence Charts make exactly this modal distinction (existential chart = sample interaction / test, `EF` in CTL*; universal chart = `AG(activation → X …)`, i.e. a response-pattern property) and Dwyer's and Autili's pattern catalogues give the closed forms, with bounded response being the universal closure of "When A, Then B within d".

### Cited Findings
- Kugler, Harel, Pnueli, Lu, Bontemps, "Temporal Logic for Scenario-Based Specifications", TACAS 2005: "existential charts can be expressed using the branching temporal logic CTL while universal charts are in the intersection of linear temporal logic and branching temporal logic LTL ∩ CTL"; "Existential charts can be used to specify system tests"; a universal chart "contains a prechart, that specifies the scenario which, if successfully executed, forces the" main chart **[source]** — [Microsoft Research PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2005/01/tacas05.pdf); [Weizmann PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2005/01/tacas05.pdf)
- Same paper, Definition 2: a system S satisfies the LSC specification iff for universal charts `L_S ⊆ L_m` and for existential charts `L_S ∩ L_m ≠ ∅`; Definition 4: universal chart `ψ_m = AG(amsg(m) → X(⋁_{w∈L^trc_m} φ^R_w))`, existential chart `ψ_m = EF(⋁ φ^R_w)`, with `φ^R_w = N U (m1 ∧ X(N U (m2 ∧ …)))` and `N = ¬e1 ∧ … ∧ ¬el`; the paper shows charts for `Fp`, `Gp`, `GFp` and `G(p → Fq)` **[source]** — [same PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2005/01/tacas05.pdf)
- Damm and Harel, "LSCs: Breathing Life into Message Sequence Charts", FMSD 19(1) 2001, 45–80, introduced universal/existential modalities and "possible, mandatory, and forbidden behavior" **[summary]** — [Weizmann wiki](https://wiki.weizmann.ac.il/playgo/index.php/Live_sequence_charts)
- Dwyer, Avrunin, Corbett (ICSE 1999): a survey of "over 500 examples of property specifications", most "instances of the proposed patterns"; patterns × scopes (globally, before, after, between, after-until) with LTL/CTL mappings **[summary]** — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Patterns-in-property-specifications-for-Dwyer-Avrunin/413baeab46e0056849585cac687a86bf9fe00b54); Response under the five scopes, e.g. After Q: `[](Q -> [](P -> <>S))`; After Q until R: `[](Q & !R -> ((P -> (!R U (S & !R))) W R)` **[source]** — [PSP LTL page](https://doi.org/10.1145/302405.302672)
- Autili et al. (TSE 2015) motivate: "specifying properties in a temporal logic is typically difficult and error-prone, even for experts in formal methods", hence patterns with a structured English grammar spanning qualitative, real-time and probabilistic variants **[summary]** — [ps-patterns.wikidot.com](http://ps-patterns.wikidot.com/); a UPPAAL instantiation exists **[summary]** — [PSP-UPPAAL](https://github.com/hub-se/PSP-UPPAAL)
- Mining of "modal scenario-based specifications from execution traces" (Lo, Maoz, Khoo) infers universal LSCs from recorded runs **[summary]** — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220883728_Mining_modal_scenario-based_specifications_from_execution_traces_of_reactive_systems); "Monitoring Systems with Extended Live Sequence Charts" (RV 2014) uses LSCs as monitors **[summary]** — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-11164-3_5)
- Gherkin→formal: 2025 work introduces "Formal Verification Gherkin Scenarios" for LLM-generated hardware BDD, translating Gherkin to properties for formal verification **[summary]** — [arXiv 2512.17814](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.17814); [arXiv 2609.15318](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.15318); natural-language→LTL benchmarks **[summary]** — [arXiv 2507.00877](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.00877)
- Quint: "a run as a first-class citizen … that can serve as a test or test pattern" **[summary]** — [quint.sh FAQ](https://quint.sh/faq)
- Bauer–Leucker–Schallhart evaluate monitorability over "The 54 monitorable LTL specification patterns" from Dwyer's catalogue **[source]** — [TOSEM PDF](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~bbonakda/teaching/CS745/papers/RV.pdf)

### Inferences
- Formally: an example `Given g, When w, Then t` denotes a finite trace `τ = g·w·t`; its existential reading `∃ run ∈ System, τ ≼ run` is Kugler et al.'s existential chart (`L_S ∩ L_m ≠ ∅`) — a test; its universal reading `∀ run ∈ System, ∀ i, (run,i ⊨ g ∧ w) → (run,i ⊨ F_[0,d] t)` is the universal chart (`L_S ⊆ L_m`) — the requirement, and with `d` it is Autili's bounded response / the reader's `withinDuration`. The reader's generator that renders examples as Gherkin and JSON fixtures (`Fixtures.lean`) is therefore producing existential charts, and the promises are the universal charts over the same vocabulary.
- An example can *witness* a co-safety property (the good prefix is in the trace) and *refute* a safety property (a bad prefix is in the trace); it can never establish a liveness property, a fairness property or a hyperproperty, by Lamport's and Clarkson–Schneider's definitions above. A set of examples can refute a k-safety hyperproperty (Agrawal–Bonakdarpour) when it contains a bad k-tuple.
- The "universal closure" of an example is precise only once the trigger (`When`) and the scope (Dwyer's *after Q / between Q and R*) are fixed; in the reader's model the scope is the run (root to last event), which is Dwyer's "Globally" over a single journey.

- No paper was found that states the example/property relation as "an example is a trace and the property is its universal closure" in those words; it is reconstructed from the LSC existential/universal semantics and the pattern catalogues.
