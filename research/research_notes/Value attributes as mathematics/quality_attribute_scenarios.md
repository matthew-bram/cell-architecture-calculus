## Key question 1: The SEI quality attribute scenario (Bass, Clements, Kazman) — six parts, use in ATAM and QAW, and the Given/When/Then correspondence

### Takeaway
The SEI form is a six-field stimulus/response record (source, stimulus, environment, artifact, response, response measure) whose "response measure" is a quantitative predicate; it is the leaf unit of ATAM's utility tree and the output unit of the QAW. No SEI source I could reach maps it to Given/When/Then; the one explicit "analogous to BDD Given/When/Then" statement I found is attributed (unverified) to Erder & Pureur's *Continuous Architecture* books, which use a reduced four-part form (stimulus, response, measurement, environment).

### Cited Findings

**Definition and six parts**
- "A quality attribute scenario is a quality attribute specific requirement with six parts: the stimulus, its source, the environment, the artifact, the response, and the response measure. It provides an operational definition for a quality attribute. For example, it's meaningless to say that a system is modifiable. Every system is modifiable with respect to one set of changes and not modifiable with respect to another. It's more meaningful to cast the requirement as a scenario, such as *A developer wishes to add an input field to the UI code at design time; modification is made with no side effect in three hours.*" — Ozkaya, Bass, Nord, Sangwan, "Making Practical Use of Quality Attribute Information", *IEEE Software* March/April 2008 [read] — [Source](https://homepages.uc.edu/~niunn/courses/RE-refs/QAS-SW08.pdf)

### Inferences
- The six SEI fields partition cleanly onto Gherkin's three clauses: **Given** = environment (+ artifact as the thing under test); **When** = source + stimulus; **Then** = response + response measure. The SEI form is therefore a *superset* of Given/When/Then with two extra typed slots (artifact; source), and with "Then" split into a qualitative response and a quantitative measure.
- The decisive difference is in the "Then": every SEI response measure in the tables is a statistic or bound over a population of runs ("average latency of two seconds", "99.999%", "85% path coverage", "detection probability"), not an assertion about one example trace. A single Given/When/Then example can check a deadline on one run; it cannot by itself express "average" or "probability" without an explicit sample.
- Neither SEI nor its principal secondary expositors (arc42, Zimmermann's DPR, Perry) draw the BDD analogy; the analogy appears in practitioner books (Continuous Architecture lineage) and in the 2024 MDPI case study, i.e. it is recognized but not canonical.

## Key question 2: Tom Gilb's Planguage — fields, quantification, examples

### Takeaway
Planguage specifies every quality as a *scalar variable* with a defined measurement scale (Scale), a measurement procedure (Meter), benchmark levels (Past, Record, Trend) and target/constraint levels (Goal/Plan, Stretch, Wish; Fail/Must, Survival/Limit), each level tagged with qualifier conditions [when, where, if]. It has no stimulus or trace; it is a measurement specification with thresholds.

### Cited Findings
- Gilb's 2004 ICEIS tutorial ("Competitive Engineering: A totally metrics-based…") lists the Planguage concepts with keyed icons: "Gist", "Ambition Level", "Scale", "Meter"; TARGETS "Goal", "Stretch", "Wish"; CONSTRAINTS "Fail", "Survival", "Limit"; SYSTEM SPACE CONDITIONS "Time, Place & Event [qualifier conditions]"; Background "Source", "Comment"; BENCHMARKS "Past", "Record", "Trend". [read] — [Source](https://iceis.scitevents.org/ICEIS2004/tutorials/gilb%20tut.pdf)
- Same tutorial, level definitions: "Past: any useful reference point. A performance or resource level achieved, in say, your old product or a competitor's organization"; "Record: best in some class, state of the art. Something to beat."; "Trend: a future estimate based on the Past"; "Limit: a level needed for system survival"; "Plan: the practical level needed for satisfaction, happiness, joy and 100% full payment!"; "Wish: a level valued by a stakeholder, but which might not be feasible. Project is not committed to it"; "Stretch: a level that is valued, yet presents a challenge to attain"; "Must: a level needed to avoid a system failure of some kind". [read] — [Source](https://iceis.scitevents.org/ICEIS2004/tutorials/gilb%20tut.pdf)
- Same tutorial, complete worked example: "Adaptability: Type: Quality Requirement. ### Inferences
- A Planguage requirement is (Scale: a measurable function of the system and qualifiers) × (Meter: an experimental protocol) × (a set of threshold levels indexed by qualifier tuples). The qualifier tuple [Task, Learner, Deadline, Market] plays the role of a "Given"; there is no "When" (no stimulus) and the "Then" is a threshold on a statistic produced by the Meter, not an outcome of a single run.
- The Meter field ("at least 10 Tasks, 5 Learner Types, 50 people") makes explicit what Given/When/Then leaves implicit: that the measure is a sample statistic. A single example cannot be a Meter.

- Gilb's own site pages (gilb.com) returned no usable text; I did not obtain the Competitive Engineering glossary verbatim. The 2005 edition details are from memory.

## Key question 3: The NFR Framework (Chung, Nixon, Yu, Mylopoulos) — softgoals, satisficing, SIGs, degree of formality

### Takeaway
The NFR Framework is a qualitative, graph-structured argumentation method: NFRs are "softgoals" without clear-cut satisfaction criteria, related by MAKE/HELP/HURT/BREAK/AND/OR contributions, evaluated by a label-propagation procedure over a Softgoal Interdependency Graph. It carries no stimulus, no measurement scale, and no numbers; later work (Yrjönen & Merilinna 2009) had to bolt on "measurable NFRs" with metrics and pass/fail values.

### Cited Findings
- Book: L. Chung, B. A. Nixon, E. Yu, J. Mylopoulos, *Non-Functional Requirements in Software Engineering*, Kluwer Academic Publishing, October 1999 (commonly cited as 2000), 472 pp., ISBN 0-7923-8666-3. The framework offers "structured graphical facilities" for NFRs; NFRs are "softgoals" that "might not be absolutely achieved" but may be "satisficed"; the "impact of decisions is qualitatively propagated" through the graphs. [summary of the authors' book page] — [Source](https://personal.utdallas.edu/~chung/BOOK/book.html)
- Chung & Leite, "On Non-Functional Requirements in Software Engineering" (Mylopoulos Festschrift, LNCS 5600, Springer 2009): "Reflecting the sense of 'good enough', the NFR Framework introduces the notion of satisficing, and, with this notion, a softgoal is said to satisfice (instead of satisfy) another softgoal. The NFR Framework offers several different types of contributions whereby a softgoal satisfices, or denies, another softgoal - MAKE, HELP, HURT and BREAK are the prominent ones, as well as AND and OR (these also incorporate the notion of 'good enough' rather than 'absolute satisfaction'). MAKE and HELP are used to represent a softgoal positively satisficing another, while BREAK and HURT to represent a softgoal negatively satisficing (or denying) another. While MAKE and BREAK respectively reflect our level of confidence in one softgoal fully satisficing or denying another, HELP and HURT respectively reflect our level of confidence in one softgoal partially satisficing or denying another." [read] — [Source](https://www-di.inf.puc-rio.br/~julio/nfr-chung-leite.pdf)
- Same paper: "In the NFR Framework, each softgoal or contribution is associated with a label, indicating the degree to which it is satisficed or denied. A label propogation procedure is offered in the NFR Framework in order to determine the effect of various design decisions … In addition to a label, each softgoal or contribution can also be associated with a criticality value, such as extremely critical, critical, and non-critical. When using the NFR Framework, NFRs are posted as softgoals to be addressed or achieved, and an iterative and interleaving process is applied in order to satisfice them, through AND/OR decompositions, operationalizations and argumentations. Throughout the process, a visual representation, SIG (softgoal interdependency graph), is created and maintained". [read] — [Source](https://www-di.inf.puc-rio.br/~julio/nfr-chung-leite.pdf)
- Same paper on linking to UML: "The central idea is to qualitatively realize softgoals in the UML models." [read] — [Source](https://www-di.inf.puc-rio.br/~julio/nfr-chung-leite.pdf)
- Same paper quotes a survey of NFR definitions that concludes "There is not a formal definition or a complete list of nonfunctional requirements." [read] — [Source](https://www-di.inf.puc-rio.br/~julio/nfr-chung-leite.pdf)
- van Lamsweerde's RE'01 tour characterizes the NFR-Framework-style softgoal: "soft goals, whose satisfaction cannot be established in a clear-cut sense [Myl92], and (hard) goals whose satisfaction can be established through verification techniques"; and the semantics: "If a goal is AND-decomposed into subgoals and all subgoals are satisficed, then the parent goal is satisficeable; but if a subgoal is denied then the parent goal is deniable. If a goal contributes negatively to another goal and the former is satisficed, then the latter is deniable. These rules are used for qualitative reasoning about goal satisficing". [read] — [Source](https://webperso.info.ucl.ac.be/~avl/files/RE01.pdf)
- Yrjönen & Merilinna (VTT), "Extending the NFR Framework with Measurable Non-Functional Requirements" (CEUR Vol-553, 2009): "To enable verification of NFRs, specific pass/failure criteria in terms of numeric values, metrics and measurement arrangements must be included. We call these measurable NFRs." Their motivation is that with the base framework "Designers might have had challenges to figure out what the requirements mean in terms of implementation and what actions are needed to achieve the desired level of satisfaction." [read] — [Source](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-553/paper2.pdf)
- [memory] SIG node types are NFR softgoals, operationalizing softgoals (design techniques) and claim softgoals (arguments); labels are satisficed, weakly satisficed, undetermined, weakly denied, denied, conflict; the book's catalogues are "type catalogues", "method catalogues" and "correlation catalogues".

### Inferences
- The NFR Framework answers a different question from Given/When/Then: not "does this run meet the bound?" but "which design alternatives contribute positively to which qualities?" Its labels are a four-or-six-valued qualitative lattice, so it is not a candidate for direct encoding as checkable examples; it is closer to a rationale/decision graph.
- Its softgoal/hardgoal split is precisely the line a proof-assistant-checked method must draw: a quality becomes checkable only once it is operationalized into a hard, measured predicate (which the 2009 VTT extension and KAOS's objective functions both do).

## Key question 4: KAOS (Dardenne, van Lamsweerde, Letier) — temporal-logic goals and the treatment of NFRs

### Takeaway
KAOS formalizes goals as real-time linear temporal logic assertions in Achieve/Cease/Maintain/Avoid patterns, with an optional formal layer; non-functional goals are specialized (accuracy, response time, throughput, confidentiality…) and, where they are "soft", are handled by qualitative satisficing. Letier & van Lamsweerde (FSE 2004) made NFRs quantitative by annotating goals with *quality variables* (random variables over a sample space) and *objective functions* (quantities with modality max/min and a target value), optionally formalized in probabilistic temporal logic.

### Cited Findings
- "A goal is an objective the system under consideration should achieve. Goal formulations thus refer to intended properties to be ensured; they are optative statements as opposed to indicative ones". [read] — van Lamsweerde, "Goal-Oriented Requirements Engineering: A Guided Tour", RE'01 — [Source](https://webperso.info.ucl.ac.be/~avl/files/RE01.pdf)
- "Functional goals underlie services that the system is expected to deliver whereas non-functional goals refer to expected system qualities such as security, safety, performance, usability, flexibility, customizability, interoperability, and so forth [Kel90]. … accuracy goals are non-functional goals requiring the state of software objects to accurately reflect the state of the corresponding monitored/controlled objects in the environment … Performance goals are specialized into time and space performance goals, the former being specialized into response time and throughput goals [Nix93]. Security goals are specialized into confidentiality, integrity and availability goals [Amo94]". [read] — [Source](https://webperso.info.ucl.ac.be/~avl/files/RE01.pdf)
- "Achieve (resp. cease) goals generate system behaviours, in that they require some target property to be eventually satisfied in some future state (resp. denied); maintain (resp. avoid) goals restrict behaviours, in that they require some target property to be permanently satisfied in every future state (resp. denied)". [read] — [Source](https://webperso.info.ucl.ac.be/~avl/files/RE01.pdf)
- "In KAOS, such assertions are written in a real-time linear temporal logic heavily inspired from [Man92, Koy92] with the usual operators over past and future states, bound by time variables; semantically, they capture maximal sets of desired behaviors [Dar93, Let01]. ### Inferences
- KAOS hard goals are the one form in this survey that is already proof-assistant-shaped: a goal is a temporal-logic formula over traces, refinement is a proved pattern, and operationalization yields pre/post/trigger conditions. A Given/When/Then example is a single trace satisfying such a formula; the formula is the universally quantified statement over all traces.
- KAOS's quantitative NFR form (quality variable over a sample space + objective function with target) is the formal counterpart of the SEI "response measure" and of Gilb's Scale/Goal, and makes explicit that the NFR is a statement about a distribution over runs, not about one run.

## Key question 5: Architectural fitness functions (Ford, Parsons, Kua) — definition, examples, ArchUnit-style tooling

### Takeaway
A fitness function is defined as "any mechanism that provides an objective integrity assessment of some architectural characteristic(s)"; the canonical examples are executable structural checks (ArchUnit layer and cycle rules) and pipeline-gated numeric thresholds (coverage, error rate, latency). It is a *checking mechanism*, agnostic about how the requirement is phrased.

### Cited Findings
- Thoughtworks Technology Radar entry "Architectural fitness function" (Trial, Nov 2017 – May 2018): "A fitness function is used to summarize how close a given design solution is to achieving the set aims"; an architectural fitness function "provides an objective integrity assessment of some architectural characteristics", encompassing unit testing, metrics and monitoring; "architects can communicate, validate and preserve architectural characteristics in an automated, continual manner, which is the key to building evolutionary architectures." - InfoQ, Thomas Much, "Fitness Functions for Your Architecture" (14 Apr 2025), quoting *Building Evolutionary Architectures* (Ford, Parsons, Kua, Sadalage): "An architectural fitness function is any mechanism that provides an objective integrity assessment of some architectural characteristic(s)". [summary with code] — [Source](https://www.infoq.com/articles/fitness-functions-architecture/)
- evolutionaryarchitecture.com (book site): first edition 2017, authors Ford, Parsons, Kua; evolutionary architectures "make it explicit what 'fit' means with as much automation as possible"; fitness functions must support "both technical and domain changes". [read] — [Source](https://www.thoughtworks.com/content/dam/thoughtworks/documents/books/bk_Fundamentals_of_Software_Architecture_Free_Chapter_en.pdf)

- Structural fitness functions (no cycles, layer rules) are universally quantified propositions over the codebase, i.e. closer to a theorem than to an example; they are the NFRs most naturally checked by a proof assistant.

## Key question 6: ISO/IEC 25010 and 25023 — characteristics, measurement functions, product quality vs quality in use

- The product-quality / quality-in-use split is the same distinction as SEI's "artifact" (system) vs. the environment-and-user context: quality in use is a measure over users and tasks and so is even further from single-trace examples.

## Key question 7: Does industry write NFRs as Gherkin scenarios? (Cucumber, Serenity BDD, Gatling, Karate) — examples and critiques

## Key question 9 (cross-cutting): Are these forms equivalent to specification by example (Given/When/Then)?

### Takeaway
Field-by-field, the SEI scenario is Given/When/Then plus two typed slots and a split "Then"; Gilb, ISO 25023 and KAOS's quantitative extension have no "When" at all and make the "Then" a statistic over a sample with a threshold. None of the forms is a single example; each is a predicate over a population of runs (or over the codebase). So an NFR fits the Given/When/Then shape only when the "Then" is allowed to quantify over many executions of the example — which is exactly how the tooling (Karate/Gatling, fitness functions) implements it.

### Cited Findings
- Field inventories (each cited in the sections above): SEI = {source, stimulus, environment, artifact, response, response measure} — [Source](https://homepages.uc.edu/~niunn/courses/RE-refs/QAS-SW08.pdf); Continuous Architecture = {stimulus, response, measurement, environment} — [Source](https://www.informit.com/articles/article.aspx?p=3128836&seqNum=3); Planguage = {Tag, Gist/Ambition, Scale, Meter, Past, Record, Trend, Goal/Plan, Stretch, Wish, Fail/Must, Survival/Limit, qualifiers [when, where, if], Source} — [Source](https://iceis.scitevents.org/ICEIS2004/tutorials/gilb%20tut.pdf); KAOS quantitative = {goal, quality variables (name, sort, sample space, definition), objective functions (name, definition, modality, target, current)} — [Source](http://www0.cs.ucl.ac.uk/staff/e.letier/publications/letier-FSE04.pdf); ISO 25023 = {ID, name, description, measurement function over QMEs} + interpretation by conformance/benchmark/time series — [Source](https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/35747/34b91bc957f647ce8bbb2093907d7bc0/ISO-IEC-25023-2016.pdf); NFR Framework = {softgoal, contribution ∈ {MAKE, HELP, HURT, BREAK, AND, OR}, label, criticality} — [Source](https://www-di.inf.puc-rio.br/~julio/nfr-chung-leite.pdf); fitness function = "any mechanism that provides an objective integrity assessment of some architectural characteristic(s)" — [Source](https://www.infoq.com/articles/fitness-functions-architecture/).
- ATAM's well-formedness condition requires exactly three things — stimulus, environmental conditions, and "the measurable or observable manifestation of the response" — [Source](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/documents/629/2000_005_001_13706.pdf); that is the When / Given / Then triple.
- Gilb's Meter mandates a sampling protocol ("at least 10 Tasks, with at least 5 Learner Types and at least 50 people") — [Source](https://iceis.scitevents.org/ICEIS2004/tutorials/gilb%20tut.pdf); Letier's quality variable is "a random variable defined over a specified sample space" — [Source](http://www0.cs.ucl.ac.uk/staff/e.letier/publications/letier-FSE04.pdf); the SEI examples use "average latency", "99.999%", "85% path coverage" — [Source](https://people.ece.ubc.ca/matei/EECE417/BASS/ch04lev1sec4.html); Karate puts `responseTime.max.lt(2000)` and `successfulRequests.percent.gt(99)` outside the feature file — [Source](https://docs.karatelabs.io/extensions/performance-testing/).

### Inferences
- Mapping: Given ← SEI environment + artifact; Gilb qualifiers [when, where, if]; Letier sample space. When ← SEI source + stimulus; absent in Gilb/ISO/Letier (they presuppose whatever workload the Meter defines). Then ← SEI response (qualitative) + response measure (quantitative); Gilb Scale + Goal/Fail; Letier objective function + target; ISO measurement function + conformance target.
- For a proof-assistant-checked method: a KAOS-style hard goal (temporal formula over a trace) is already a theorem over all traces and a Given/When/Then example is one witness; a quantitative NFR needs an explicit sample (a finite multiset of traces or a distribution) as part of the "Given", and the "Then" becomes a statement about a statistic of that sample. Structural NFRs (no cycles, layering) need no trace at all and are plain propositions about the artifact.
- The honest answer to "equivalent?": the *shape* is the same (context / trigger / observable outcome), the *quantifier* is not (one example vs. a population or the whole artifact). Treating an NFR as a Given/When/Then with a sample-valued Given and a statistic-valued Then preserves both.

### Gaps
- No source found that performs this mapping formally; the inferences are mine, built on the cited field inventories.
