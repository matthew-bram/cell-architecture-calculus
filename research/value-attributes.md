# Quantifiers sort value attributes into provable families

Yes, there is a real opportunity, and it is sharper than "describe non-functional requirements mathematically": every value attribute the method cares about falls into one of four mathematical families decided by *what its statement quantifies over*, and that quantifier fixes both which Lean 4 machinery can prove it and what shape a Given/When/Then example must take to check it. Timing deadlines, ordering, counts, "never after" and authorization are properties of **one trace** (safety properties in LTLf/MTL); they are exactly the shape of the method's existing `RunPromise`, decidable on a single recorded run, and provable in Lean 4 core with `omega` and `decide`. Confidentiality is a **2-safety hyperproperty** that needs a pair of runs; cross-run service levels ("99% of runs within an hour") and the method's own kept-over-seen measure are hyperproperties over **sets of runs**; reliability under a model, expected latency and differential privacy quantify over **distributions** and need Mathlib's `ℝ≥0∞`, `PMF` and measure theory. The zone-indexed identifiers and the actor gate already deliver the first layer of information-flow security (the forbidden flow is unwritable; every decision is mediated), but the method still owes a two-run noninterference theorem over an explicit semantics of its commands, a delimited-release statement for `scrub`, and the Cedar-shaped trio (explicit permit, default deny, order independence) for the gate. A single example witnesses a co-safety property and refutes a safety property; it cannot establish liveness, a hyperproperty or a distributional claim, so a faithful confidentiality example is a *pair* of journeys and a faithful SLA example carries a *sample* in its Given. A per-cell non-functional contract is an assume/guarantee pair per viewpoint sharing one assumption; latency folds as sum along `seq` and max across `par` (a 60-line Lean 4 core proof compiled against the project toolchain), unavailability budgets sum along both constructors because `par` is all-must-succeed, cost sums, and confidentiality needs hypercontracts rather than trace-set contracts.

## Four quantifiers, not nine ISO characteristics, partition the attributes

The standards and architecture literature organise quality attributes by *topic*. ISO/IEC 25010:2023 names nine product-quality characteristics (functional suitability, performance efficiency, compatibility, interaction capability, reliability, security, maintainability, flexibility, safety) and moved quality-in-use to a separate standard ([ISO/IEC 25010:2023 sample](https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/78176/13ff8ea97048443f99318920757df124/ISO-IEC-25010-2023.pdf)); the SEI quality-attribute scenario has six fields (source, stimulus, environment, artifact, response, response measure) and insists that "it's meaningless to say that a system is modifiable" until the scenario is concrete ([Ozkaya et al., IEEE Software 2008](https://homepages.uc.edu/~niunn/courses/RE-refs/QAS-SW08.pdf)). Neither taxonomy tells you what mathematics an attribute needs. The partition that does comes from Clarkson and Schneider: a *trace property* is a set of traces, a *hyperproperty* is a set of sets of traces, and "important security policies cannot be expressed as properties of individual executions". Their own worked example is the one that matters for value attributes: a bound on the maximum response time in each execution is a trace property, but "the mean response time over all executions is less than 1 second" is not, and it is hyperliveness rather than hypersafety ([Clarkson & Schneider, Hyperproperties](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/fbs/publications/Hyperproperties.pdf)). Lamport supplies the second axis: a property is safety iff every violation is witnessed by a finite prefix, liveness iff every finite prefix can be extended to satisfy it, and every property is a conjunction of the two ([Lamport, Safety, Liveness, and Fairness](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/tla/safety-liveness.pdf)). Put together, these give four families with different quantifiers and different proof obligations.

| Family | Quantifies over | Attributes that live here | Fitting logic | Verdict on one recorded run | Where the method already stands |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trace property, safety | one finite run | deadline ("B within d of A"), ordering, count per run, count per window, "never after", authorization ("this actor is refused"), per-run cost total | LTLf / pointwise MTL over a timestamped word; MFOTL with `CNT` for windows | two-valued, decidable | `withinDuration`, `atMostCount`, `neverAfter`; the actor gate |
| Trace property, liveness | one infinite behaviour | "eventually responds" without a bound, fairness (`GF`, TLA+ WF/SF) | LTL, TLA+ | inconclusive (`?` in LTL3); `GFp` is non-monitorable | nothing, by design (runs are finite) |
| Hyperproperty | pairs or sets of runs | confidentiality (noninterference, observational determinism), robust declassification, cross-run SLA (mean, p99, "99% within d"), robustness to small input changes | HyperLTL `∀π∀π′…` for k-safety; set-level definitions for hyperliveness | needs ≥2 runs; a bad k-tuple refutes k-safety; a ratio needs the whole population | `kept : List Run → Nat × Nat`; the zone index as the definition of low-equivalence |
| Distributional | a distribution over runs | reliability under a model (`P≥0.99 [F≤1 "served"]`), expected latency, steady-state availability of a CTMC, differential privacy, sample confidence | PCTL/CSL with rewards; (ε,δ)-DP; Hoeffding | not checkable on any finite set of traces; only estimated or proved about the mechanism | nothing yet |
| Structural | the artifact itself | no dependency cycles, layering, "every zone-A record is built from `Id A`" | plain propositions; type-formation rules | no run needed | the zone-indexed types, the DAG compilation theorem |

The method's three promises sit squarely in the first row. `withinDuration` is the bounded-response pattern `G(root → F_[0,limit] target)` read on `recordedAt` and `issuedAt`; `atMostCount` is bounded existence; `neverAfter` is Dwyer's "absence after Q" pattern `[](Q -> [](!P))` ([Dwyer et al., property specification patterns](https://doi.org/10.1145/302405.302672)). All three are safety properties, so they are correctly two-valued on a terminated run, and the finite-trace semantics they use is LTLf's, where "strong next" is false at the end of a trace and "weak next" is true ([Vin et al., LeanLTL, ITP 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01780)); `neverAfter` returning true when `find?` finds no fence is the weak reading. The one place the method already steps out of the first row is `kept`, which counts how many of a set of runs satisfied a promise: a threshold on that ratio ("≥ 99% of runs within d") is Clarkson and Schneider's "time service factor, the percentage of requests that are serviced within a specified time", and it is a hyperproperty over the set, not an `all` of a per-run predicate ([Clarkson & Schneider](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/fbs/publications/Hyperproperties.pdf)).

The structured NFR forms confirm the partition from the other direction. The SEI six fields map onto Gherkin cleanly, Given = environment plus artifact, When = source plus stimulus, Then = response plus response measure, but every response measure in the SEI tables is a statistic over a population ("average latency of two seconds", "85% path coverage", "detection probability"), not an assertion about one trace ([Bass, Clements, Kazman, ch. 4](https://people.ece.ubc.ca/matei/EECE417/BASS/ch04lev1sec4.html)). Gilb's Planguage goes further and has no "When" at all: a quality is a Scale, a Meter that mandates a sampling protocol ("at least 10 Tasks, with at least 5 Learner Types and at least 50 people"), and threshold levels (Goal, Fail, Survival) indexed by qualifier tuples ([Gilb, ICEIS 2004 tutorial](https://iceis.scitevents.org/ICEIS2004/tutorials/gilb%20tut.pdf)). KAOS's quantitative extension says it outright: a goal's partial satisfaction is modelled by "quality variables corresponding to domain-specific, goal-related random variables defined over specified sample spaces" and objective functions such as `P(RespTime ≤ 14') Max 95%` ([Letier & van Lamsweerde, FSE 2004](http://www0.cs.ucl.ac.uk/staff/e.letier/publications/letier-FSE04.pdf)); KAOS's *hard* goals, by contrast, are real-time temporal-logic formulas in Achieve/Maintain/Avoid patterns with proved refinement patterns ([van Lamsweerde, RE'01](https://webperso.info.ucl.ac.be/~avl/files/RE01.pdf)), which is the first row again. The NFR Framework's softgoals, "satisficed" rather than satisfied and propagated through MAKE/HELP/HURT/BREAK labels ([Chung & Leite](https://www-di.inf.puc-rio.br/~julio/nfr-chung-leite.pdf)), are not checkable at all until operationalised into a measured predicate, which the VTT extension had to add ([Yrjönen & Merilinna, 2009](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-553/paper2.pdf)). Architectural fitness functions, "any mechanism that provides an objective integrity assessment of some architectural characteristic(s)", are the structural row when they are ArchUnit rules and the hyperproperty row when they are pipeline thresholds on error rate ([InfoQ, Fitness Functions](https://www.infoq.com/articles/fitness-functions-architecture/)). One terminological note: "value attributes" is not a term of art in any of this literature; the closest lineage is Gilb's "Value Requirements", a superset of quality requirements, with Richards and Ford's "architecture characteristics" and the SEI/ISO "quality attributes" as the standard names ([Gilb, BCS 2024](https://www.bcs.org/media/sqxjw0up/ai-better-requirements-tom-gilb.pdf)).

## The zone index makes the bad flow unwritable; the theorem is still owed

The method's security construction has a name and a theorem. Identifiers indexed by a trust zone (`Id z`), so that a zone-A record built from an `Id B` is a type error, are the "labels as type indices" pattern of information-flow control: Denning's lattice of security classes ([Denning, CACM 1976](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/360051.360056)) turned into a type system by Volpano, Smith and Irvine, who proved "that all well-typed programs have the noninterference property" ([VSI, JCS 1996](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/JCS-1996-42-304)), and encoded with dependent types in DepSec, where "the security level of a type depend[s] on runtime values" ([Lourenço & Caires, POPL 2015](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/2775051.2676994); [DepSec](https://cs.au.dk/~gregersen/papers/2019-post-depsec-full.pdf)). In Denning's vocabulary the zones are pairwise incomparable classes, so the policy is "no flow between zones except through `scrub`". The theorem that pattern corresponds to is **noninterference**: two inputs indistinguishable to a low observer yield indistinguishable outputs ([Sabelfeld & Myers, JSAC 2003](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/andru/papers/jsac/sm-jsac03.pdf)), and it "is not a trace property but rather a 2-safety hyperproperty" ([Clarkson & Schneider, CSF 2008](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/fbs/publications/HyperpropertiesCSFW.pdf)). The single `scrub` whose type names two zones is a **declassification** at a fixed *where*; Sabelfeld and Sands classify declassification by what/who/where/when, and the matching policy is *delimited release*, which "stipulates that information may only be released through escape hatches and no additional information is leaked" ([Sabelfeld & Sands, JCS 2007](https://www.cse.chalmers.se/~andrei/sabelfeld-sands-jcs07.pdf)). Because commands carry an actor, the *who* dimension applies as robust declassification ("attackers cannot selectively trigger declassification of chosen secrets through their inputs"), and its modern combined form with transparent endorsement, nonmalleable information flow, is a 4-safety hyperproperty ([Cecchetti, Myers, Arden, CCS 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.08596)). Finally, the policy check before a decision is a **reference monitor** in Anderson's sense, and the property it must satisfy is Saltzer and Schroeder's *complete mediation* ([Schneider, reference monitors chapter](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/fbs/publications/chptr.enfMech.refMonitor.pdf)). The private-zone-plus-scrub design is also GDPR Article 4(5) in types: pseudonymisation is processing such that data "can no longer be attributed to a specific data subject without the use of additional information, provided that such additional information is kept separately" ([GDPR Art. 4](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-4-gdpr/)).

What the strong typing already covers is precise: it is **layer one** of a two-layer argument. The index makes the forbidden term unwritable, and if a `Decision` is constructible only from evidence `Policy.allows actor cmd`, then "every decision was authorised" holds by construction, the same shape as Cedar's "explicit permit required". What it does not cover is layer two: the theorem *about* the typed language that every writable program is noninterferent. In the literature that theorem is proved once, by a logical relation or parametricity, about the host type system; MAC proves "a scheduler-parametric, progress-sensitive non-interference theorem" in Agda ([Vassena, Russo et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S235222081730069X)), Gregersen et al. prove termination-insensitive noninterference with a Kripke logical relation in Coq on Iris ([POPL 2021](https://pure.au.dk/portal/en/publications/mechanized-logical-relations-for-termination-insensitive-noninter/)), and Bowman and Ahmed show the connection to parametricity in System F ([Noninterference for Free](http://www.ccs.neu.edu/home/amal/papers/nifree.pdf)). Lean 4 does not hand the method that argument for free: there is no established, peer-reviewed Lean 4 formalisation of a security type system with a noninterference theorem (the only hits were a self-published repository and a mid-2026 arXiv paper whose mechanisation tool is unconfirmed), and whether Lean's `Type` admits a parametricity-style "free" noninterference argument is unsettled. The practical route is the explicit one: give the model's commands a small-step or big-step semantics in Lean and prove that for all states `s₁ ≈_A s₂` (equal on zone-A observables) every well-formed command yields `≈_A` results. In that theorem the zone index on `Id` serves as the *definition* of `≈_A`, not as the proof. "Iris in Lean" (September 2026) makes a binary logical-relation argument feasible in Lean 4 with Mathlib ([arXiv 2609.24252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24252)), which it was not a year earlier.

The owed theorems come in four pieces, of three different shapes. **Authorization** is one-run safety: make `Decision` constructible only from `allows` evidence, then prove Cedar's trio. Cedar's Lean development proves forbid-overrides, "explicit permission required", default deny, and order/duplicate independence, and depends only on `batteries`, not Mathlib ([cedar-spec/cedar-lean](https://github.com/cedar-policy/cedar-spec/tree/main/cedar-lean)); the Cedar paper's statements ("If no `permit` policy evaluates to `true`, the request is denied"; "If a request is allowed, some `permit` policy evaluated to `true`") are the template ([Cedar, OOPSLA 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.04651)). **Confidentiality** is two-run: the `≈_A` theorem above, then re-proved with `scrub`'s outputs added to the equivalence, which is delimited release with `scrub` as the only hatch. **Robustness** (the *who*) is one-run again: prove that no command whose actor lacks private-zone trust can reach `scrub`, which with the two-run theorem yields robust declassification. **Pseudonym quality** is a separate statement about `scrub` itself: that a deterministic pseudonym leaks the equality relation on identities across records, so either that equality is a declared hatch or per-context pseudonyms are used; the ICO's guidance that pseudonymisation "is only effective if the key and the pseudonymised data are stored separately" names the risk without formalising it ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-sharing/anonymisation/pseudonymisation/)). Two warnings from the literature apply directly. First, "refinement does not preserve hyperproperties" ([Clarkson & Schneider](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/fbs/publications/HyperpropertiesCSFW.pdf)), so a noninterference theorem on the model does not transfer to an implementation by ordinary refinement; Cedar's answer is a proven executable model plus differential random testing of the production code, which found "nearly two dozen bugs" the proofs did not ([Cedar](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.04651)). Second, authorisation and information flow "can subvert each other's security": a refusal is itself information, so the decision needs a zone label and policy changes are flows ([Arden, Liu, Myers, FLAM](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/entities/publication/a0ee6cf5-6c73-4c53-9715-1203ad1ae1dd)).

Privacy as a *number* is a different family altogether. Differential privacy bounds how much one individual's presence shifts an output distribution; it "is a relational property of programs (also known as a hyperproperty)" but over distributions, so no finite set of traces witnesses a violation and it cannot be checked on recorded runs at all ([NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10061759)). SampCert is the worked Lean 4 example: "over 12,000 lines of proof", generic over pure, concentrated and Rényi DP, verified discrete Laplace and Gaussian samplers, "heavy use of Lean's extensive Mathlib library", and deployed in AWS Clean Rooms ([SampCert, arXiv 2412.01671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01671); [repository](https://github.com/leanprover/SampCert)). It is the right dependency only if aggregates ever leave the private zone; for identifiers and records, delimited release is the statement.

## An example witnesses one trace and cannot see a pair or a distribution

A Given/When/Then example is one finite trace, and the requirement it illustrates is the universal closure of that trace over all runs. Live Sequence Charts make exactly this modal distinction: "existential charts can be expressed using the branching temporal logic CTL while universal charts are in the intersection of linear temporal logic and branching temporal logic", and "existential charts can be used to specify system tests", with the universal chart `AG(activation → X …)` being the requirement ([Kugler, Harel, Pnueli, Lu, Bontemps, TACAS 2005](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2005/01/tacas05.pdf)). In the method's terms the fixtures generator that renders examples as Gherkin and JSON produces existential charts, and the promises are the universal charts over the same vocabulary. Quint says the same from the tooling side, treating "a run as a first-class citizen, which is a concrete execution path that can serve as a test or test pattern" ([Quint FAQ](https://quint.sh/faq)).

From Lamport's definitions the limits follow directly. An example can **witness** a co-safety property (the good prefix is in the trace) and **refute** a safety property (a bad prefix is in the trace). It can never **establish** a liveness property, since "any finite sequence of states can be extended to a behavior satisfying" `◇(x = 3)` ([Lamport](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/tla/safety-liveness.pdf)); runtime verification formalises this as the `?` verdict, returned when "the so far observed prefix u itself is insufficient to determine how ϕ evaluates in any possible future continuation" ([Bauer, Leucker, Schallhart, TOSEM 2011](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~bbonakda/teaching/CS745/papers/RV.pdf)), and `GFp` fairness is non-monitorable outright ([Agrawal & Bonakdarpour, CSF 2016](https://www.cse.msu.edu/tart/sites/default/files/publications/files/2023-10/csf16.pdf)). For a terminated run read with LTLf semantics, which is what `RunPromise` does, the verdict is two-valued and the `?` never arises; a three-valued `Verdict` is needed only if runs are treated as open prefixes. TLA+ trace validation is the complementary check, membership rather than property: Cirstea, Kuppe, Loillier and Merz reduce "is this recorded trace a prefix of some behaviour of Spec" to constrained model checking, deliberately ignore liveness because it "would not hold for incomplete prefixes", and found "serious violations of key safety properties" in CCF this way ([arXiv 2404.16075](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16075)).

Hyperproperties push the limit one step further: a single example cannot even refute one. Observational determinism is "a 2-safety hyperproperty" whose "bad thing is a pair of" traces ([Clarkson & Schneider](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/fbs/publications/Hyperproperties.pdf)), so the minimal faithful confidentiality example is a *pair*: "Given two private zone states differing only in identity, When the same journey runs, Then the outside-zone observations are equal". A single "the id is not shown" scenario is a symptom check, not a specification. Sets of examples do work for k-safety: Agrawal and Bonakdarpour give a three-valued semantics of HyperLTL over a finite set of finite traces and prove that every k-safety hyperproperty is monitorable, while `∀∃` formulas are not "as evaluating such formulas requires one to have all traces of the system" ([CSF 2016](https://www.cse.msu.edu/tart/sites/default/files/publications/files/2023-10/csf16.pdf)). The authorisation side is the mirror image and is good news: a misuse case, "a function that the system should not allow" with *threatens/mitigates* links ([Sindre & Opdahl](https://www.se.rit.edu/~se555/Misuse%20Cases.pdf)), written as "Given actor X, When command C, Then refused", is one trace with a decidable predicate, so it is a well-formed specification by example of a safety property and runs directly as a differential test against any implementation, which is how BDD-Security and Gauntlt use Gherkin ([bdd-security](https://github.com/iriusrisk/bdd-security); [gauntlt](https://github.com/gauntlt/gauntlt)).

Quantitative attributes need a sample in the Given. Industry tooling already does this by splitting the layers: Karate reuses *unchanged* functional feature files as Gatling load scripts and puts the NFR assertions, `global.responseTime.max.lt(2000)` and `global.successfulRequests.percent.gt(99)`, in the simulation class with the injection profile, not in Gherkin ([Karate performance testing](https://docs.karatelabs.io/extensions/performance-testing/)). Nicieja's judgement that "conveying nonfunctional requirements in Gherkin isn't an established practice" ([Writing Great Specifications](https://livebook.manning.com/concept/software-development/nonfunctional-requirement)) is about this loss of outside-in character, not about the logic. The honest decomposition of an SLA such as "99% within an hour over 30 days" has three parts: a per-run Boolean predicate (a trace property, which the promise already is), a population (the multiset of runs that started in the window), and a measure (the ratio ≥ 0.99). Statistical model checking formalises the bridge when the population is sampled rather than fully observed: "each run of the system is encoded as a Bernoulli random variable", the question `Pr[bound](φ) ≥ p0` is answered by Wald's sequential test with an indifference region, and estimation sample sizes come from the Chernoff–Hoeffding bound ([Legay et al., UPPAAL-SMC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.1272)). Solving that bound for an SLO target of 0.99 at half-width 0.005 and 95% confidence needs roughly 74,000 runs, which is why production SLOs are computed on the full population and why `kept` over the actual window is the right primitive. Differential privacy is the one attribute no example can state at any arity, because it is a bound on distributions ([NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10061759)).

## Lean 4 core reaches counts and bounds; Mathlib owns distributions

The dividing line between core and Mathlib runs exactly along the quantifier families. Everything in the trace-property row and the observed-population reading of the hyperproperty row is `Nat`/`Int` arithmetic over `List`, and `omega`, in core since Lean 4.7, "decides any system of linear inequalities over Nat or Int, handling +, -, multiplication by constants, ≤, <, =, ≠, and logical combinations" with `/` and `%` by literals ([Lean 4.8.0 release](https://lean-lang.org/blog/2024-6-1-lean-480/)). The method's own `withinDuration_mono`, proved with `simp only [List.any_eq_true, …]` and `omega` in a package whose `lakefile.toml` declares no dependencies, is the existence proof. Everything in the distributional row needs `ℝ≥0∞`, which is `WithTop NNReal`, which needs `Real`: Mathlib's `PMF α` is `{f : α → ℝ≥0∞ // HasSum f 1}` with a monad and a `toMeasure` bridge into measure theory ([Mathlib PMF](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/ProbabilityMassFunction/Basic.html)), and SampCert's Mathlib dependence is not incidental, since both the statement (a bound on a ratio of probabilities) and the proof (the Poisson summation formula) require it ([SampCert repository](https://github.com/leanprover/SampCert)).

The ecosystem survey behind the table is thin on the Lean side and that is itself a finding. Lean 4 has three LTL libraries, LeanLTL (finite and infinite traces, LTLf embedding, Mathlib-dependent), Lentil (TLA-style proof mode, depends only on `aesop`) and LeanearTemporalLogic, and none has metric or bounded operators ([LeanLTL](https://github.com/UCSCFormalMethods/LeanLTL); [Lentil](https://github.com/verse-lab/Lentil); [LeanearTemporalLogic](https://github.com/mrigankpawagi/LeanearTemporalLogic)). Isabelle has the only verified metric first-order monitors, with aggregations and a sliding-window algorithm ([Basin et al., IJCAR 2020](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-51074-9_25)), the only mechanised probabilistic model checker, and an HOL4 formalisation of availability block diagrams ([Ahmad & Hasan](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.01755)). Assume/guarantee contracts are mechanised in Coq ([Kastenbaum, Boyer, Talpin, ICTAC 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13647)), network calculus in Isabelle and Coq ([Mabille et al., ITP 2013](https://members.loria.fr/SMerz/papers/itp2013.pdf)), and no Lean 4 formalisation of any of these exists as of October 2026. The two Lean 4 bright spots are choreographic programming, mechanised twice in 2025–26 with endpoint-projection soundness and deadlock freedom ([Mech](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15174); [Böhler et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16346)), and the single cost-bound project lean4-camus, which carries paper-level complexity claims through a cost monad with Mathlib ([lean4-camus](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-camus)). The method does not need to import any of these for the first-row work; it needs Mathlib the moment it says "probability" or "expected" about future runs rather than "fraction" about observed ones.

## A per-cell contract is one assumption and five guarantees that fold along the journey

The published theory for "how does a journey's property follow from its cells' properties" is the assume/guarantee contract algebra, and it is small enough to state. A contract is a pair (A, G) of assertions; for saturated contracts refinement is "A2 ⊇ A1 and G2 ⊆ G1" (weaker assumption, stronger guarantee), conjunction is "(A1∪A2, G1∩G2)", and parallel composition has the closed form "G = G1∩G2 and A = (A1∩A2) ∪ ¬(G1∩G2)" ([Benveniste et al., Contracts for System Design](https://www.cpsschool.eu/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/main_contracts.pdf)). Non-functional viewpoints are not special in this algebra: "full specification of a component can be a conjunction of multiple viewpoints, each covering a specific (functional, timing, safety etc.) aspect", and the monograph's own timing example is a contract guaranteeing `tz ≤ max(tx, ty) + 1`, literally the fan-in rule. The example also exposes the one pitfall: a timing contract with no assumption "inherits the weakest assumption" under conjunction "and cancels the assumption of C1", so every viewpoint must repeat the cell's input assumption. Refinement is preserved by composition (Lemma 4.2), and sub-distributivity "[(C11∧C21)⊗(C12∧C22)] ⪯ (C11⊗C12) ∧ (C21⊗C22)" means implementing each cell against the conjunction of its viewpoints and then composing yields the conjunction of the journey-level viewpoints ([monograph](https://www.cpsschool.eu/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/main_contracts.pdf)). Confidentiality is the exception: it is a hyperproperty, so trace-set contracts cannot state it, and hypercontracts are the published extension, "subsuming simulation preorders" and illustrated on security ([Incer et al., NFM 2022](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~sseshia/pubdir/nfm22.pdf)).

A per-cell non-functional contract for this method therefore contains one shared **assumption** and one guarantee per viewpoint. The assumption is the decider's acceptance predicate on inputs, plus the input arrival rate or window count if queueing is in scope, plus the environment failure events the cell tolerates (the chaos-engineering "vary real-world events" list, which is a test of the assumption side, not the arithmetic ([principlesofchaos.org](https://principlesofchaos.org/))). The **latency guarantee** is a deterministic bound d, or a rate-latency curve β_{R,T} if the cell queues, optionally with a percentile pair (p, d_p). The **availability guarantee** is an unavailability budget u with the independence assumptions made explicit, since the SRE Workbook warns the parallel formula "assumes that both services are wholly independent, which is almost never the case" ([SRE Workbook, Implementing SLOs](https://sre.google/workbook/implementing-slos/)). The **cost guarantee** splits as resource interfaces do into accumulated cost per handled event and peak resource ([Chakrabarti et al., Resource Interfaces](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-45212-6_9)). The **confidentiality guarantee** is the hypercontract: noninterference between zones stated over named observers. The **refinement obligation** closes the loop: a cell may tighten any guarantee or weaken its assumption freely without breaking any journey; any other change re-derives every journey's bound.

Composition along the journey algebra is then a fold, and the rules differ by attribute. For deterministic latency, bound(step) = d, bound(seq a b) = bound a + bound b, bound(par a b) = max(bound a, bound b). This is network calculus with pure-delay service curves: the concatenation theorem says a flow through S1 then S2 "offers a service curve of β1 ⊗ β2", and for rate-latency curves "concatenating Intserv nodes amounts to adding the latency components and taking the minimum of the rates" ([Le Boudec & Thiran, Network Calculus](https://leboudec.github.io/netcal/latex/netCalBook.pdf)). The same source carries the warning for the queued case, "pay bursts only once": the end-to-end bound from the global service curve is strictly better than the sum of per-node bounds, so a cell that queues should expose (latency, rate), not a single number. With unbounded parallelism, which the `par` semantics assumes, the fold is the DAG's critical path, the degenerate case of Graham's bound ([He et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08800)). The fold has been proved in Lean 4 core: a 60-line file defining `bound`, a semantic `elapsed` under an environment, the assumption `StepsHonoured` (every mentioned step's actual duration is within its contract), and the theorem `elapsed_le_bound` by `induction … with` and `omega`, together with `bound_tighten_le` for the refinement direction, compiled with `lean LatencyBound.lean` at the project's Lean 4.34.1 with no imports and no warnings (`LatencyBound.lean`, kept under `research_notes/`). Attaching it to the real `Journey State Refusal` needs a contract field on `step` or a side map keyed on `name`, and the DAG compiler's node list can carry the same bound as the longest path over `needs`, so the two presentations agree by `compile` correctness.

Percentiles do not fold. Along `seq` the distribution is a convolution and across `par` the fan-in CDF is a product of branch CDFs, which is the tail-at-scale amplification: with 100 servers each slow one time in 100, "63% of user requests will take more than one second", and Google's measured 99th percentile grows from 10 ms for one random leaf to 140 ms for all leaves ([Dean & Barroso](https://www.barroso.org/publications/TheTailAtScale.pdf)). The only percentile rule that stays in Lean core is the union bound: carry (p, d) per step with p as failures per million and prove P(elapsed > Σd) ≤ Σ(1 − p) by linear `Nat` arithmetic; anything tighter needs the distribution and Mathlib. Availability folds the opposite way from what the word "parallel" suggests: `par` in this algebra is *all-must-succeed* (both branches feed `merge`), so it is series for availability, and only an explicit "either branch suffices" construct, which the algebra lacks, earns 1 − Π(1 − A_i). Unavailability budgets therefore add along *both* constructors to first order, which is why Google reasons in budgets and why "the rule of the extra 9", a critical dependency must be about ten times more reliable than the service, is the refinement discipline that keeps per-cell budgets negligible relative to the journey's ([Treynor Sloss et al., The Calculus of Service Availability](https://research.google/pubs/the-calculus-of-service-availability/)). Cost sums along both constructors; peak resource folds as max along `seq` and sum across `par`, the dual of latency. Confidentiality composes by conjunction only when both cells' hypercontracts are stated over the same observers. The microservice literature confirms this is the right shape by its absence: formal choreography work proves deadlock-freedom and saga atomicity, never timing or availability bounds ([Accompanist](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20942)), while the SRE literature propagates SLOs informally along dependency graphs ([Sedlak et al., SOSE 2024](https://dsg.tuwien.ac.at/~sd/papers/SOSE_2024_B_Sedlak.pdf)); the DAG compilation theorem already gives the safety half, and the latency and budget folds over the same term are the missing other half.

## Conclusion

The deepest result of the survey is that the method's existing structures already have the right mathematical shapes, and the work is to name them and finish the proofs rather than to import a framework. `RunPromise` is an LTLf safety predicate; `kept` is a hyperproperty measure (the time service factor); the `Journey` fold is the parallel composition of contracts with wiring; the zone index is the definition of low-equivalence for a noninterference theorem the method has not yet stated; the actor gate is a reference monitor whose Cedar-shaped trio is a few small lemmas away. The single design decision that remains is to attach a quantifier to every attribute before writing its example, because the quantifier decides whether the example is one trace, a pair, a sample with a ratio, or no example at all.

The ordering of work follows from how much each theorem costs. Authorisation and the latency, count and budget folds are core-only and provable now; the two-run confidentiality theorem is the one that changes the method's security claim from "unwritable" to "proved" and should be stated over an explicit command semantics before any Iris machinery is considered; distributional claims, including anything that says "probability", "expected" or "ε", should be deferred until a release of aggregates across zones actually exists, at which point SampCert and Mathlib's `PMF` are the dependencies, not before. Two cautions carry across everything: refinement does not preserve hyperproperties, so the scrub round-trip and replay checks complement a model theorem rather than substitute for it; and `par` is series, not redundant parallel, for every attribute except latency, which is the one place the fold's intuition and the word "parallel" diverge.
